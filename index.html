Unit 3 Grammar Overview

Welcome to Grade 3 English! In Unit 3, we explore two core grammar structures: Grammar 1 (Simple Past: Yes/No Questions & Short Answers) and Grammar 2 (Quantifiers: too, too much, too many, and enough). Let's review the rules below like a classroom whiteboard!

Grammar 1

Simple Past: Yes/No Questions

We use Did + subject + base verb to ask about completed actions in the past.

Question: Did + [you / he / she / they] + [brush / make / do] + ...?
Affirmative Answer: Yes, [I / he / she / they] + did.
Negative Answer: No, [I / he / she / they] + didn't.
Grammar 2

Quantifiers: too, too much, too many, enough

Expressing amounts and limits in daily habits and routines.

  • too + adjective: Don't stay up too late!
  • too much + uncountable noun: Eat too much junk food.
  • too many + countable noun: Play too many video games.
  • enough + noun / verb + enough: Get enough sleep / get enough exercise.

Resolved Examples (15 Total: 5 Basic, 5 Intermediate, 5 Advanced)

Study these step-by-step examples designed for Third Grade students following National Geographic's Our World 3 curriculum.

⭐ Basic Level Examples (1 - 5)

Example 1 (Grammar 1): Asking about brushing teeth.

Prompt: Did you brush your teeth?

Step-by-step Solution: Identify subject ("you") and base verb ("brush"). Answer affirmatively: Yes, I did.

Example 2 (Grammar 1): Asking about making a bed.

Prompt: Did he make his bed? (Negative)

Step-by-step Solution: Subject is "he". For a negative response, use "didn't". Answer: No, he didn't.

Example 3 (Grammar 1): Asking about homework.

Prompt: Did they do their homework? (Affirmative)

Step-by-step Solution: Subject is "they". Affirmative response uses "did". Answer: Yes, they did.

Example 4 (Grammar 2): Using "too late".

Prompt: Don't stay up ______ late! (too / enough)

Step-by-step Solution: Before an adjective ("late") indicating excess, we use too. Answer: too.

Example 5 (Grammar 2): Using "exercise".

Prompt: I play soccer every day. I get ______ exercise. (too / enough)

Step-by-step Solution: Positive habit with adequate quantity uses enough before the noun. Answer: enough.

⭐⭐ Intermediate Level Examples (6 - 10)

Example 6 (Grammar 1): Formulating Past Questions.

Prompt: Formulate question: she / walk / to school?

Step-by-step Solution: Start with auxiliary "Did" + subject "she" + base verb "walk" + complement "to school?". Answer: Did she walk to school?

Example 7 (Grammar 1): Past question with time marker.

Prompt: Formulate question: you / play baseball / yesterday?

Step-by-step Solution: Auxiliary "Did" + subject "you" + verb phrase "play baseball yesterday?". Answer: Did you play baseball yesterday?

Example 8 (Grammar 2): Too much vs Too many.

Prompt: Complete: I eat ______ junk food. (too much / too many)

Step-by-step Solution: "Junk food" is an uncountable noun, so we use too much. Answer: too much.

Example 9 (Grammar 2): Countable noun quantifier.

Prompt: Complete: I play ______ video games. (too much / too many)

Step-by-step Solution: "Video games" is a plural countable noun, so we use too many. Answer: too many.

Example 10 (Grammar 2): Drink enough water.

Prompt: Complete: I drink ______ water every day. (too / enough)

Step-by-step Solution: "Water" is a noun, and adequate amount uses enough. Answer: enough.

⭐⭐⭐ Advanced Level Examples (11 - 15)

Example 11 (Grammar 1): Picture interpretation dialogue.

Prompt: Look at the park scene: Did she throw the ball?

Step-by-step Solution: Observing the image, the girl is holding and throwing a baseball. Affirmative short answer: Yes, she did.

Example 12 (Grammar 1): Negative action analysis.

Prompt: Look at the park scene: Did he catch the ball?

Step-by-step Solution: The boy is holding a glove or missing the catch. Negative short answer: No, he didn't.

Example 13 (Grammar 1): Plural question construction.

Prompt: Formulate and answer: children / ride bikes to the park? (Negative)

Step-by-step Solution: Question: Did the children ride their bikes to the park? Answer: No, they didn't.

Example 14 (Grammar 2): Combining sleep habits.

Prompt: Complete with correct quantifier: Do you get ______ sleep at night? (too much / enough)

Step-by-step Solution: Healthy requirement for rest uses enough sleep. Answer: enough.

Example 15 (Grammar 2): Combining vegetables and health.

Prompt: Complete sentence: I eat ______ vegetables every day to stay strong. (too many / enough)

Step-by-step Solution: Eating a healthy, sufficient quantity of vegetables uses enough. Answer: enough.

✏️ Basic Exercises

1. Fill in the blank with Did or didn't:

______ you brush your knees? Yes, I ______.

Solution: Did you brush your knees? Yes, I did.

2. Complete the short answer:

Did she play in the park? Yes, she ______.

Solution: Yes, she did.

3. Complete the negative short answer:

Did you eat breakfast? No, I ______.

Solution: No, I didn't.

4. Choose too or enough:

Don't stay up ______ late!

Solution: too (too late).

5. Choose too or enough:

I get ______ exercise every day.

Solution: enough (enough exercise).

✏️ Intermediate Exercises

6. Write the correct question word and form:

______ he ______ his back? (No, he didn't.)

Solution: Did he hurt (or scratch / wash) his back?

7. Match / Fill in the blank:

Did they do their homework? No, they ______.

Solution: No, they didn't.

8. Choose too much or too many:

Don't watch ______ much TV!

Solution: too (too much TV). Note: TV is uncountable here as screen time.

9. Choose too much or too many:

I drink ______ much soda.

Solution: too (too much soda). Soda is uncountable.

10. Answer based on personal habit:

Do you eat enough fruit every day? (Write Yes or No)

Solution: Sample answer: Yes, I do. (or No, I don't.)

✏️ Advanced Exercises

11. Construct a full question from prompts:

Prompts: you / eat yogurt for breakfast?

Solution: Did you eat yogurt for breakfast?

12. Picture analysis:

Did the children ride their bikes to the park? (Based on Our World 3 park scene)

Solution: No, they didn't. (Skateboards are shown on the picnic blanket instead!).

13. Distinguish Quantifiers:

Why do we use too many with video games and too much with junk food?

Solution: Because "video games" is countable (plural noun), while "junk food" is uncountable (mass noun).

14. Complete the health table:

Do you get enough sleep at night? Check Yes or No and write a sentence.

Solution: Yes, I do. I sleep 9 hours every night. (or equivalent personal answer).

15. Transform affirmative to question:

Transform: "He played soccer yesterday" into a Yes/No past question.

Solution: Did he play soccer yesterday? (Note: "played" changes back to base verb "play" after "Did").
